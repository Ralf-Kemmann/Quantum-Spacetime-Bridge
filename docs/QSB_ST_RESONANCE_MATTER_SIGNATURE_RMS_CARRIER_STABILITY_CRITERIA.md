# QSB-ST Resonance Matter Signature
## RMS Carrier and Stability Criteria

This note gives QSB-ST a positive next discipline: define what could count as a bridge-relevant RMS carrier, what remains a marker or diagnostic, and which stability tests must be passed before stronger language is used.

Guiding principle: "Die Idee ins Licht. Die Grenzen ans Geländer. Die Details in den Maschinenraum."

## 1. Purpose

This is a theory/architecture note for QSB-ST Resonance Matter Signature. It defines candidate RMS carriers and stability criteria.

It is not a result note and not a proof that RMS is physically established. It defines how the project should distinguish carrier, marker, diagnostic, and readout layers, and what stability conditions a candidate RMS carrier must satisfy before it can be treated as bridge-relevant.

## 2. Why carrier and stability criteria are needed

QSB-ST currently treats RMS as a candidate bridge-relevant structure, not as an established physical carrier. The project has useful readouts across geometry, phase, Matter Signature, Carbon calibration, VDW, isotope, QC, shell, valence, and negative/abs channels. Those readouts should not be collapsed into one physical interpretation.

The strongest current carrier hypothesis should be framed cautiously as:

phase-coherent off-diagonal relational correlation structure

or, in a more formal possible route:

density-matrix / ODLRO-like off-diagonal order candidate.

These are candidate routes. The carrier role is not established by this note.

## 4. Carrier versus marker versus diagnostic

Definitions:

- Carrier: a structure that could physically or informationally carry bridge-relevant organization.
- Candidate carrier: a plausible carrier under investigation, not yet established.
- Marker layer: a readout or feature that may track bridge-relevant organization without being the underlying carrier.
- Diagnostic: a computed quantity used to test stability, specificity, readability, or mimicry.
- Readout: a numerical or structural output of a pipeline.

Examples:

- `K_ij`: correlation / Gram object; possible carrier context but not automatically carrier.
- `|K| / G`: amplitude or magnitude layer; marker/readout unless phase role is established.
- `arg(K)`: phase matrix; candidate contributor.
- phase-coherent off-diagonal relational structure: candidate carrier.
- density matrix `rho` / off-diagonal density-matrix structure: formal candidate carrier route.
- ODLRO-like structure: analogy / candidate formal route, not established.
- `D_rel`: marker layer / diagnostic readout unless physically anchored.
- negative/abs marker channels: marker layer; shared upper block under tested controls, not established as the physical carrier.
- VDW / isotope / QC / shell / valence axes: candidate matter-signature modifiers; surrogate-level unless canonically rerun and anchored.
- Carbon diagnostics: scanner calibration and mimicry discipline, not carrier proof.

## 5. Candidate RMS carrier hierarchy

### Level 0: Pipeline Readouts

Examples: `D_rel`, `geometry_readability`, core recovery, containment, phase influence.

These are useful diagnostics, not carriers.

### Level 1: Marker Layers

Examples: negative/abs channels, VDW score layers, Matter Signature axes, Carbon scaffold diagnostics.

These may reveal or track structure, but they are not physical carriers by default.

### Level 2: Candidate Relational Carrier

The main candidate is phase-coherent off-diagonal relational correlation structure.

This level requires stability, specificity, and geometry-anchor compatibility before it can support stronger bridge language.

### Level 3: Formal Physical Candidate

Possible route: density-matrix / ODLRO-like off-diagonal order or another formal quantum-information object.

This level requires a precise operator/density-matrix formulation, open-system/decoherence behavior, and external observable anchors.

### Level 4: Established Carrier

Not reached.

This level would require derivation, stability, external anchoring, reproducibility, and compatibility with RT/QM constraints.

## 6. Phase-coherent off-diagonal relational structure

The phase-coherent off-diagonal relational correlation structure hypothesis says that the bridge-relevant organization, if present, should live in relational phase-coherent off-diagonal structure rather than in a single scalar score.

This is a candidate carrier hypothesis because it could connect phase organization, relational correlation, geometry readability, and RMS behavior. It remains a hypothesis until it survives amplitude decoupling, strong nulls, mimicry tests, geometry-anchor conditions, and external anchoring.

## 7. Density-matrix / ODLRO-like candidate route

A more formal possible route is to express the candidate carrier through a density-matrix object or an ODLRO-like off-diagonal order criterion.

This route would require:

- a precise definition of the state space;
- a valid density matrix `rho`;
- a defined relation between `K_ij`, off-diagonal structure, and RMS readouts;
- open-system and decoherence behavior;
- entropy or coherence diagnostics;
- external observable anchors.

ODLRO-like language should remain analogical or candidate-formal unless the operator, limit, and observable consequences are specified.

## 8. Matter Signature axes as candidate modifiers, not established carriers

Matter Signature axes can modify, reveal, or stress-test the RMS candidate. They should not be treated as carriers by default.

Current status:

- isotope axes: candidate mass/nuclear-parameter modifiers;
- QC / shell / valence axes: candidate descriptor modifiers;
- VDW axes: candidate matter-sensitive modifiers;
- negative/abs channels: marker layers with a shared upper block under tested controls;
- Carbon diagnostics: scanner calibration and mimicry discipline.

These axes become stronger only when they are canonically rerun, anchored, and shown to contribute structure beyond mass, amplitude, label, topology, score mixing, and pipeline effects.

## 9. Stability criteria

### 1. Perturbation stability

The candidate structure persists under controlled small phase/amplitude/noise perturbations.

### 2. Phase stability

Relative phase organization remains nontrivial and structurally relevant.

### 3. Amplitude-decoupling stability

The signal cannot be explained by amplitude/magnitude structure alone.

### 4. Backbone / pipeline stability

Core or readout stability survives backbone-method variation, threshold variation, edge-count neighborhoods, and cost-function variation.

### 5. Null-model stability

The candidate outperforms strong controls:

- random phase;
- trivial phase;
- spectrum-matched random phase;
- energy/momentum-shuffled phase;
- amplitude-shuffled / phase-preserved controls;
- label/family shuffles;
- covariance-preserving nulls;
- mimicry controls.

### 6. Coarse-graining stability

The structure remains meaningful or transforms predictably under scale changes.

### 7. Decoherence tolerance

The candidate has a defined response to off-diagonal loss or environmental noise.

### 8. External-anchor stability

When tied to geometry or interaction anchors, the relationship survives pipeline variation and null models.

## 10. Specificity criteria

Specificity must be separated into several forms:

- phase-specificity: the signal depends on phase organization rather than magnitude alone;
- de-Broglie specificity: the de-Broglie phase structure is distinguished from structured alternatives, not only random/trivial controls;
- carrier specificity: the candidate carrier explains the readout better than marker layers;
- matter-signature specificity: matter axes add structure beyond mass-only, score-mixing, or label effects;
- anti-mimicry specificity: the readout resists synthetic, shuffled, and mimic controls.

Random/trivial controls are necessary but not sufficient for de-Broglie specificity. Structured non-de-Broglie and spectrum-matched controls are required.

## 11. Geometry-anchor compatibility criteria

This note connects directly to the Geometry Anchor Conditions note.

A candidate carrier becomes stronger if its readouts correlate with independently anchored `D(A,B)`, Green/Poisson-readable regimes, or declared scaling laws. Geometry readability alone is not carrier proof. The geometry anchor must remain separate from the carrier hypothesis until external observables, scaling behavior, and null models support the connection.

## 12. Causality / entropy compatibility criteria

This note also connects to the Causality and Entropy Anchor Note.

A candidate carrier should eventually be tested for:

- relational delay ordering;
- asymmetric perturbation response;
- no-signalling compatibility;
- entropy / mutual information / coherence readouts;
- decoherence sensitivity;
- coarse-graining behavior.

These tests would examine whether the candidate becomes causally or entropically readable, not whether correlation alone produces causality or entropy.

## 13. Failure and warning modes

Failure and warning modes include:

- amplitude dominance mistaken for phase carrier;
- `D_rel` mistaken for carrier;
- negative channel overprivileged despite negative/abs ambiguity;
- VDW score mixing mistaken for carrier axis;
- Carbon scanner calibration overread as carrier proof;
- pipeline-generated cores;
- label/topology leakage;
- weak null models;
- post hoc carrier selection;
- multiple-comparison accumulation;
- archival result treated as canonical result.

These warnings should be reported as boundary findings rather than hidden.
