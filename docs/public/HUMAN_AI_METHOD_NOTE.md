# Human–AI Collaboration Method Note

## Why this note exists

QSB uses AI assistance extensively. Hiding that fact would make the research less transparent, not more credible.

The collaboration is therefore described as a method, with explicit boundaries.

## Human responsibility

The human researcher remains responsible for:

- selecting the research question;
- deciding which physical assumptions are admissible;
- interpreting equations and numerical results;
- accepting or rejecting models and controls;
- authorising runs and claim changes;
- approving public wording;
- checking sources and references;
- deciding authorship and submission;
- taking responsibility for every published scientific statement.

AI output is not treated as scientific authority.

## Machine-supported work

AI systems may assist with:

- structuring research questions and work packages;
- drafting and reviewing code;
- generating deterministic validation routines;
- transforming and documenting data;
- checking units, dimensions, field names, and consistency;
- comparing run artifacts and status contracts;
- locating contradictions or missing metadata;
- preparing tables, figures, summaries, and editorial drafts;
- maintaining continuity across a large audit trail.

These activities remain subject to human review and repository evidence.

## Operating principle

The project combines different strengths:

> Human intuition, meaning, and judgement on one side; machine-supported structure, formal logic, repetition, and consistency control on the other.

Progress is attributed to the collaboration, not to an autonomous machine discovery.

## Anti-hallucination controls

The repository workflow reduces unsupported statements through:

- source and version tracking;
- explicit run contracts;
- deterministic scripts where possible;
- preserved command logs and outputs;
- validation tables;
- unit and dimension checks;
- negative and neutral result retention;
- separate human reviews;
- claim-boundary files;
- frozen scientific cores before editorial rewriting.

When evidence is missing, the correct result is a documented gap or blocked status.

## Authorship and disclosure

AI systems are not listed as authors. They cannot accept responsibility, approve a manuscript, disclose conflicts, or answer for the integrity of the work.

Each QSB publication should include a concise disclosure adapted to the venue. A suitable baseline is:

> AI systems were used as research-assistance tools for software development, data structuring, consistency checking, documentation, and editorial support. Research questions, physical interpretation, validation decisions, claim boundaries, and final manuscript approval remained the responsibility of the human author.

Specific tools and their roles can be listed where the journal or platform requires it.

## Style and personal voice

Editorial assistance must not replace the author’s voice with generic AI prose.

The external English should remain recognisably consistent with the way the author explains and discusses the work: direct, guided, understandable, and cautious without becoming timid. The purpose of editing is to improve technical English and precision, not to invent a different speaker.
