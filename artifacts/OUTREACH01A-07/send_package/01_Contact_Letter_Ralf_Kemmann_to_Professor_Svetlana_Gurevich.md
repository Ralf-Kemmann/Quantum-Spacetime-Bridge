Professor Svetlana Gurevich

Dear Professor Gurevich,

My name is Ralf Kemmann. I am a Diplom-Chemiker with a natural-science background shaped by theoretical chemistry, inorganic chemistry and solid-state questions. Alongside that training I have worked for many years in software development, data architecture, statistics and scientific modelling. I now work as an independent researcher on QSB, a data-driven and relational research framework.

The methodological issue isolated in the attached material lies close to several questions studied by your group: nonlinear dynamical systems, delayed systems, phase-shifted equivalent states, domains and boundary structures. The material is not presented as a model of the physical systems studied by your group. I am also not asking the group to assess the wider QSB programme. The attached package isolates one technical question concerning state identity, dynamic equivalence, temporal phase position and boundary representation.

The package contains, for orientation, a short technical note, a compact three-record table and a visible figure. The dataset is synthetic: two declared dynamically equivalent state records, separated by one drive-period phase offset, plus one open boundary record. Its purpose is bookkeeping clarity, not a physical claim.

What I would value is a limited technical reaction: whether this separation of record identity, dynamic equivalence, phase position and boundary representation is useful, trivial, misleading or incomplete for discussing such states.

I am seeking only a brief technical assessment of the representation itself; no broader commitment is implied. A brief indication that the question falls outside the group’s scope would also be helpful.

Sincerely,
Ralf Kemmann
