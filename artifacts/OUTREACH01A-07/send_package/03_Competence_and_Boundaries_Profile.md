# Competence and Boundaries Profile

## 1. Scientific Background

I am a Diplom-Chemiker. My scientific background is rooted in theoretical chemistry, inorganic chemistry and solid-state chemistry, with an emphasis on structure, measurable constraints, state descriptions, transformations and the distinction between an interpretation and a result. This is not a claim of established expertise in laser physics or discrete time-crystal experiments. It is a statement of training and working orientation.

My chemistry background matters here because it brings a habit of separating composition, structure, state, phase, boundary and evidence level. I use that habit in the synthetic dynamical-state demonstrator included in this package, while keeping the physical limits explicit.

## 2. Computational and Data Background

My computational side is long-standing: software development, scientific data processing, data architecture, statistics, schema work, validation logic and reproducible pipelines. My practical emphasis is on making assumptions visible and keeping data, rules and interpretation apart.

This includes work with structured records, controlled fields, transformation checks, reproducible scripts, validation reports and audit trails. The strength is not that a small script proves a scientific claim. The strength is that the script makes the claim boundary inspectable.

## 3. Current Independent Research Work

My current work is independent research on QSB, treated here as a data-driven, relational and reproducible framework. The wider QSB programme is broader than the present demonstrator, but it is not being submitted for assessment in this contact material.

I isolate one small question in the present package: how to represent two declared dynamically equivalent but non-identical records, their phase positions, their domains and an open boundary representation without turning that representation into a physical assertion.

## 5. Relevant Strengths

My relevant strengths for this contact are specific: formal state descriptions, relational bookkeeping, statistical and computational modelling, reproducible computational workflows, schema-like validation, careful separation of presentation views from canonical data, and defensive scientific writing.

That combination is useful for preparing a small technical question. It does not replace domain expertise in nonlinear laser systems. It does not establish that the representation is adequate in the context of the Gurevich group’s work.

## 6. Clear Boundaries

My background does not include established expertise in laser physics, an experimental DTC programme or independent validation of QSB, and I do not claim an institutional affiliation in this field. The contribution I can offer lies elsewhere: structured state representation, reproducible data handling and a disciplined separation of evidence from interpretation.

The package asks only for technical criticism of the representation; it does not request validation, endorsement, supervision or collaboration.
